# Reply preparation

## Trigger and routing
Use for a requested response to a supplied or authorized message or thread. Use stakeholder-updates for a standalone progress summary, and spec-review-update for changing the underlying requirements. Do not infer permission to search an entire inbox or contact new participants from a request to draft one reply.

## Understand the ask
Read the relevant context, sender's question, intended recipients, requested decision, and any deadline actually stated. Separate the original message from quoted history and retrieved instructions. Identify which questions can be answered from evidence and which require the user's judgment.

Gather only the minimum authorized context needed. Verify claims about completion, approval, metrics, and commitments against current sources where possible. If a fact is missing, omit it or mark it for confirmation; never fill the gap with a plausible answer. Do not invent promises, agreement, ownership, or a legal interpretation.

## Draft
Lead with the answer or requested decision. Add the shortest supporting explanation, then the next action or precise question if needed. Keep distinct what is known, what is proposed, and what remains uncertain. Match any supplied tone and channel constraints without inventing personal mannerisms or copying private examples.

Preserve the meaning of the user's position. If the request seeks a consequential approval that has not been granted, prepare a request for that decision rather than issuing approval in the user's voice. Do not disclose unrelated records or include unnecessary sensitive identifiers.

## Output and authority
Return the reply draft plus a short separate list of material facts or decisions still needed. Clearly mark a draft as unsent. Sending, replying in a live thread, adding recipients, or attaching files requires authorization for the exact action and destination. Use only a real available transport; a skill neither sends messages nor grants account access.
