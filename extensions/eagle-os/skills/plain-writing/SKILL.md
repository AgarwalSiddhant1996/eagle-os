# Plain writing

## Trigger and limit
Use when drafting reader-facing prose or reviewing it for clarity. This is an editing pass, not permission to change facts, remove necessary caveats, adopt an identity, or send a message.

## Before writing
Identify the audience, channel, purpose, length constraint, and the action or decision the reader needs. Use the user's supplied voice preferences rather than inventing personal mannerisms. If there is no special voice request, use clear, neutral language.

## Edit in order
1. Lead with the result, request, or decision. Put supporting context after it.
2. Keep one idea per paragraph. Prefer short concrete verbs and named actors when known.
3. Replace vague phrases such as "drive meaningful outcomes" with the actual change and its consequence. Remove ceremonial openings, repetitive summaries, inflated adjectives, and transitions that add no information.
4. Separate observations, interpretations, and proposals. Preserve uncertainty and cite the source of consequential claims. Do not invent dates, owners, numbers, quotations, links, or commitments.
5. Retain the details needed to act: what changed, who is responsible if known, the real deadline if supplied, and the specific next step.
6. Read once for meaning and once for unnecessary words. Keep specialist terminology when simplifying it would lose precision; explain it briefly for a non-specialist audience.

## Final checks
Can the intended reader identify the point and next step without rereading? Does every strong claim have support? Did brevity remove a necessary condition? Is the draft honest about what is incomplete?

Remove sensitive details not required by the audience. Never turn confidential material into a public example. Show requested copy as a draft; obtain approval before publishing or sending unless the exact action was already authorized. If asked only to edit, return the edited text without a long explanation of the edits.
